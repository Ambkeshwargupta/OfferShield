Trust and limitations

How evidence works

OfferShield is designed to help people investigate, not to issue an automated verdict.

What a company record tells you

A government registry may establish that a legal entity is recorded, and provide details associated with it. It does not prove that a person contacting you works for that entity or that a vacancy exists.

What a matching domain tells you

A domain may match an address published by the company. This cannot inspect message headers, detect spoofing, confirm mailbox control, or prove that a recruiter is authorised.

What company hiring guidance tells you

Company-owned recruitment advisories are useful first-party evidence about published channels and policies. They may not describe every business unit, geography, role or exception.

What missing information means

“Not found” means the source set used here did not contain the information. It is not evidence of fraud. Smaller businesses may have a limited public footprint.

Suggested independent verification steps

  1. Open the company's official website by typing its domain yourself, not by clicking an unsolicited link.
  2. Use the contact or recruitment channel published on that official website.
  3. Ask the company to confirm the recruiter's name, role, vacancy reference and offer status.
  4. Check legal identifiers directly on the relevant government portal.
  5. Preserve the message and transaction details if you suspect fraud. Use the official cybercrime portal or helpline 1930 where appropriate.
